Development 2 — how a genome becomes a body

A guided read of lib/devo2.js. Every body on this page is grown by the same develop() the world calls at each birth.

0. What a genome is here

Not a body, and not a wiring diagram. A genome is a small gene regulatory network: a recipe for a process that runs inside an egg and leaves a body behind.

the numbers

one gene's share of the genome


      
Sparse on purpose. A dense 64-gene network would be 4,096 weights
      and evolution would explore none of it, so each gene listens to just
       others and structural mutation moves those edges around.

    

  


  
The previous encoding (devo.js) painted each cell's properties
  as a weighted sum of nine fixed functions of position. It had no time, no signalling and
  no growth — and measured over 64 living genomes it produced bodies of elongation
  p50 = p90 = max = 1.15, identical to random founders. The map could reach
  elongation 4.0 with hand-made genomes; evolution never found it. That failure is why
  this model exists.






  
1. The egg, and what the mother provides

  
A cell cannot know where it is unless something tells it. The mother lays
  down gradients in the egg; every cell reads them as the first few gene concentrations,
  which it cannot change.


  

    

      
      

        
        
        
        
      

    

    

      
why these four

      
ap and dv give an axis and a
      side. rad tells a cell whether it is in the interior or at the surface.
      noise is fixed per site and is the only thing that distinguishes two
      otherwise identical positions.

      
Noise matters more than it looks. A perfectly symmetric network in a
      perfectly symmetric egg stays symmetric forever — there is nothing to break the tie.
      Real embryos have the same problem and solve it the same way.

      
There is also crowd, which is not maternal: it is
      how many neighbours a cell currently has. That is the one input that changes as the
      body grows, and it is what lets the network know it has finished.

    

  






  
2. One gene, on its own

  
Every gene product is a concentration that is produced and decays. This is
  the whole dynamical law, quoted from the module:


  
dc_g/dt = P * sigmoid(bias_g + SUM_k w_gk * c_src(g,k))  -  decay_g * c_g
                                                          +  D_g * laplacian


  
CTRNN-shaped — the same gather-and-squash — but concentrations cannot go
  negative, and decay is what oscillators are built from. Move the sliders and watch a
  single gene with no regulators find its equilibrium.


  

    

      
    

    

      
      
      
Equilibrium is where production balances decay:
      c* = sigmoid(bias) / decay. A strongly negative bias switches the gene
      off; a large decay makes it fast and faint. Neither is a mode — both are just
      numbers in the genome.

    

  






  
3. Two genes that hate each other

  
Mutual inhibition is the motif that breaks symmetry, and it is not a
  special case in the code — it is simply w_ab < 0 and w_ba < 0.
  Two cells starting almost identically end up committed to opposite states.


  

    

      
    

    

      
      
      
With weak inhibition both settle to the same middling value and the
      cells stay interchangeable. Past a threshold the tie breaks and the tiny initial
      difference — in the real model, noise — decides which gene wins.

    

  






  
4. Diffusion, and why the rates differ

  
Without transport each cell runs its network alone and the only spatial
  information is the maternal gradient — the old model with extra steps. Patterns need a
  slow activator against a fast inhibitor, so diffusion is per-product and
  evolved, spanning two decades.


  

    

      
    

    

      
      
At a ratio near 1 the two spread together and smooth each other into a
      flat field. Raise it and bands appear at a spacing the ratio sets — nobody wrote a
      stripe count anywhere.

      
This is the mechanism available for segmentation. Whether
      evolution in this world has actually used it is a separate question, and the honest
      answer measured so far is mostly no.

    

  






  
5. Sixteen eggs, one clock

  
Sixteen different genomes, each starting as a single cell at the centre of
  its own egg. Press play and watch them all develop together. Nothing here needs tuning —
  the only control is time.


  


  

    

      
      
      
    

    

    

      sensor
      muscle
      neuron
      anchor
    

    

      
      click any embryo to carry it into the next steps
    

  


  
Every egg is drawn at the same scale, so what you are watching is size
  changing, not an arrangement changing. Development is canalised — the noise is
  derived from the genome — so each of these is one fixed process being replayed, not a
  fresh roll each time you scrub.


  
The world only ever runs the first 12,000 ms of this, with a
  60-cell cap. These bodies keep going to about 18,000 and reach a mean of 156 cells. The
  animals in the live world are early stages of a process that was stopped, not finished
  organisms — and the reason is cost, not biology: every cell diffuses to its neighbours
  each tick, so development is quadratic in cells.


  
Watch for stripes. There mostly are not any. Measured across 40
  random genomes, counting sign changes in contractility along the long axis: mean 1.0 bands
  at 12,000 ms, 0.9 at 30,000, 0.27 at 60,000, one genome in forty reaching seven. One
  band is head-and-tail polarity, not segmentation. Elongation FALLS as they grow — 1.58,
  1.23, 1.09 — so they get bigger and rounder, filling a circular egg rather than extending
  along an axis. The mechanism for banding exists and step 4 shows it working in isolation.
  Evolution in this world has not used it.






  
6. Carving, not only building

  
Shape does not have to come only from where growth happened. The
  survive gene decides whether a cell is still part of the body when the egg
  hatches — embryos remove as much as they add, and interdigital webbing is deleted rather
  than never grown.


  
There is a specific reason this exists. Patterning across a field — stripes,
  bands, gap-gene domains — needs a field to pattern: a fly reads its gradients across
  thousands of nuclei that already exist. A body extruded one cell at a time from a growing
  tip never has that field, which is the leading suspect for why segmentation measures near
  zero under pure tip growth. Cleavage fills the egg so a field exists, and
  survive is what turns a filled egg into a shape rather than a disc.


  

    

      
    

    

      
      

      
Low threshold keeps almost everything and you get the disc back. High
      threshold carves too hard and the body falls below the minimum size to live. The
      interesting shapes are in between, which is where evolution has to find them.

    

  






  
7. What a cell ends up being

  
Fifteen of the sixty-four gene products are read out as the cell's
  properties. These are continuous capacities, and the kernel uses them
  directly.


  

    
the readout genes

    

      
label versus capability

      
A cell is labelled by whichever of
      sense, contract and grip is largest, with a
      floor below which it is "just tissue". That label is a summary for humans.

      
It used to be more than that. The kernel branched on it, so a
      cell whose grip beat its sense by 0.008 could not sense at
      all. Measured over 64 living genomes, the labelling was discarding 47% of the
      contractility in the tissue and 42.7% of the sensing, and sixteen of sixty-four
      bodies were functionally blind while carrying sensory tissue. All three gates are
      gone; a cell now senses, contracts and grips in proportion to what it has.

      

    

  






  
8. Mutation, and what it does to a body

  
Perturb the genome and re-grow it. Small changes to weights usually shift a
  body; changes to which gene listens to which can restructure it.


  

    

      
      
parent

    

    

      
      
mutant

    

    

      
      
      

      
Watch how often a mutation produces no body at all. Development can
      fail — run out of yolk, or specify something too small to live — and the parent simply
      loses the investment. That is a normal outcome, not an error.

    

  






  
9. Expert topics

  
The parts that are easy to get wrong, and the measurements behind them.


  

    

      
canalisation

      
Developmental noise is derived from the genome, so one
      genome grows one body every time. Before that, within-genome sd was 0.264 against
      between-genome 0.084 — heritability 0.093 — and directed selection on morphology ran
      downhill, because it was chasing lucky rolls rather than heritable shape.

    

    

      
synapses are expressed, not inherited

      
A synapse's weight is not stored per edge. It is computed
      from what its two endpoint cells are made of and where they sit. Copying a parent's
      edge table instead would mean the brain is inherited as a literal wiring diagram while
      the body develops — so a child whose body came out different would be wearing its
      parent's connectome.

    

  


  

    

      
why the label is not the capability

      
A cell is labelled by whichever of sense, contract and grip
      is largest. The kernel used to branch on that label, and the cost was measured: 47% of
      the contractility in the tissue unusable, 42.7% of the sensing discarded, 424 cells
      losing the argmax by under 0.10, and sixteen of sixty-four bodies functionally blind
      while carrying sensory tissue. One specimen had cells with sense 0.992 labelled anchor
      because grip came out at 1.000 — a margin of 0.008 deciding that an animal could not
      see. All three gates are gone.

    

    

      
a whole world of sensors reading noise

      
All three sense channels are written as
      mix(noise, signal, acuity), and acuity comes from the gene
      senseTune. Nothing ever wired senseTune to the sense gene, so
      acuity was zero for every cell in the entire history of this world —
      every sensor read pure noise, and since the energy cost also scales with acuity,
      sensing was free at the same time. Free, universal and informationless.

    

  


  

    

      
what is deliberately absent

      
No segment count. No limb primitive. No symmetry flag. The
      structures worth having are meant to be reachable through the operator, never
      arranged by it — the difference between making an outcome possible and imposing it,
      which is the mistake this codebase has repeatedly had to undo.

    

    

      
the honest state

      
Bodies of ~13 cells with about 1.8 distinct cell types.
      Multicellularity is taxed: energy per cell falls monotonically with body size, measured
      at −0.4041 ± 0.0669. Differentiation needs spare cells, so until size pays
      there is nothing to differentiate with. That is the blocker, and it is economic rather
      than developmental.
