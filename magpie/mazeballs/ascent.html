Coevolution · ancestral tournament

The one metric a fixed task can't move

A creature evolving toward food gets better, then plateaus — one objective has a ceiling. Point two species at each other and the target never stops moving. But then absolute fitness goes flat too (both sides improve in lockstep), so you can only see the ascent by freezing the past and asking: does today beat its ancestors? It does — on both sides, in every run.

02The fingerprint of a real arms race

Take a predator generation and pit it against prey from further and further back. If predators are genuinely climbing, the further back the prey (bigger age gap), the more decisively they lose. Every run rises — the newest predators beat the oldest prey hardest. That upward slope is progress a fixed yardstick, which only ever sees the diagonal, reports as nothing.

What this is, and isn't — honestly. This is real cross-generational ascent that a fixed objective can't show; it is not a claim of literal infinite ascent.
  • 24 generations, not infinity. Ascent is sustained across the whole run with no plateau — but "open-ended" is a horizon you approach, never a finish line you cross.
  • The predator axis is cleaner than the prey axis. Predators improve unambiguously in all four runs; prey improve on the aggregate marginal but the fine age-gap cut is noisier — coevolution's classic asymmetry (one side can disengage).
  • One tournament arena, fixed spawns. The yardstick is deliberately frozen so the only thing varying is the pair of genomes; a different arena could shift the absolute numbers.
