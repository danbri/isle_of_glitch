A run-analytics page, and two devo viewers that speak devo2

10 August 2026 · posts, viewer, analytics, devo, coordination

Notes for the other session. All of this is in world.html and a new analytics.html; none of it touches the sim libs, so it sits on top of your devo2/world_gpu rework without conflict. I rebased onto it and checked: the genome layout, the DevoView API, and the packMeta bit-packing the cell colour decodes are all unchanged, so the colour, gene viewer, network view and unfold still match your libs.

The gene viewer and unfolder are devo2 now

  • Unfolder — the Creature panel's unfold ▶ runs the real model in lib/devo-worker.js and draws it with DevoView, growing in developmental time, instead of the old order-only animation. The 2D division-tree overlay is gone; if you want the mother→daughter links you added to DevoView, they can come back into this view for free.
  • Gene viewer — works without a server (reads this page's own living genomes and the devo2 layout off the module constants) and shows the full 64-gene GRN.
  • GRN network view — a table/network toggle. The network is the wiring diagram: genes as nodes in three bands (egg gradients and external channels in from the left, outputs on the right, internal regulators in the middle), edges coloured amber for excitation and blue for repression. Tap a gene and its regulators and targets light up, with its wiring read out in words; tap again to clear.
  • Egg panel — egg size and development time are sliders that re-run the unfold, so you can change the environment and watch the same genome build a different body. Heat, sugar-energy and agitation are marked as the egg factors still to come.

Terminology: the viewer and the dev-viewer docs now say egg gradients, not maternal. They are a provisioned environment the egg sets, not a signal from a parent.

The CTRNN scope stops hogging and flatlining

It was opening a 200px full-width panel over the world on every selection. It now opens compact on a narrow screen (control bar only; the choice is remembered), the default height is smaller, and a genuinely quiet brain is drawn as one line of text rather than a wall of flat traces.

analytics.html — population dynamics from the committed archives

A standalone page, linked from the home hub, that reads runs/archive/gen-*.json directly. It draws four things over an evolutionary run: the population trajectory (alive / lineages / mean energy), a body-size strip plot per generation split by whether a lineage senses and moves, the cell-type composition band, and the sensorimotor fraction over time. The data is one representative per lineage sampled every few generations — trends, not a census — and the page says so.

Two things worth knowing:

  • New artifact: runs/archive/index.json, a manifest of the archive filenames. A static host cannot list a directory, so the page needs it to enumerate the files. It is committed and currently regenerated by hand; if archiveIfDue() appended each new filename to it, the page would stay current on its own.
  • No d3. The page is hand-drawn 2D canvas in the same idiom as drawScope. Reasoning: the site serves committed files with no build step, self-contained pages are the norm here, and these chart types are a few hundred lines in the existing style. d3 stays a future option — vendored as a committed file, never a CDN — only if network or Sankey views are wanted later.

One thing the data already says: the sensorimotor fraction across the archived run drifts from about 0.66 at generation 5 to 0.44 at 129. Worth a proper look against the metrics ledger before reading anything into it.

The dense series (births/deaths, the body-size min/median/max envelope, failed eggs) lives only in runs/metrics.jsonl, which is gitignored and has no HTTP route. A read-only /metrics route on the server would let analytics.html?watch=1 show it live — the obvious next step if the static page proves useful.
