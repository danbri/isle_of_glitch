Development 2.0 is what grows the bodies now

6 August 2026 · posts, devo, grn, integration, live

The GRN-in-an-egg is wired into the live world. world.html is running on it, verified in a browser rather than inferred from a passing test.

devo 2  devo2-grn  eggExtent 12
steps 126,504   alive 1,200   births 1,554   deaths 654
generation 7    lineages 267  bodies 5/21/60
failedEggs 43 (plateaued)     NaN 0   errors 0

What had to be true first

The brain nearly came out dead. evolve.js wires every synapse with synapse(genome, cellA, cellB), reading a block of coefficients Dev 2.0 genomes simply did not have — the function would have read past the GRN into zeros and returned SYN_RANGE * tanh(0) for every edge in the population. This repo has a commit named "the brains were dead", so that failure mode is not hypothetical. Dev 2.0 now carries the block, seeded and mutated at the same magnitudes Dev 1.0 uses, and imports SYN_BASIS and SPACING from devo.js rather than restating them. A duplicated SPACING has already cost this codebase every bond in every body once; twice would be a choice.

The egg size had to travel with the encoding. evolve.js had eggExtent = 3.0 hardcoded, which is right for Dev 1.0 — it samples a fixed disc — and catastrophic for Dev 2.0, which grows into open space and needs 12. Handing it 3.0 rebuilds the disc-shaped bodies the whole encoding exists to escape, and it does so silently: bodies still appear, they are just all round. So the extent is now a property the encoding declares, not a constant the caller remembers.

Division became a rate

Growth was polled every fifth integrator step against a threshold. That is a timescale nobody chose, and it divided the whole embryo on the same tick because the loop said so.

Now each cell accumulates readiness at a rate set by its own grow output and divides when it crosses 1, then resets. Division is a rate — a cell expressing grow twice as hard divides twice as often — and cells fall out of phase with each other on their own.

founder viability 92% -> 100% median body 25 -> 51 cells segments (max) 0 -> 6

Segments appearing at all is the first sign of patterning in this encoding.

Cleave mode: built, measured, and currently worse

Prompted by a good question — do we fill the egg with pluripotent cells, or add them by division as development runs?

Real embryos mostly do the first, and it matters more than it sounds. Drosophila patterns a syncytial blastoderm of ~6000 nuclei that already exist; the gap-gene network reads gradients across a field. A body extruded one cell at a time from a tip never has a field, which is the leading suspect for why segments sat at zero under pure tip growth.

So cleave mode fills the egg, patterns it, then sculpts with a survive gene — apoptosis, the way a hand is made rather than grown.

It produces discs.

mode                       viable  satur  elP90  elMax  segMax
grow                         100%    15%   1.15   2.31       6
cleave (any cleaveFrac)       97%    96%   1.15   1.23       0

96% saturate the cell cap. The cause is mundane: survive settles at sigmoid(bias)/decay, which sits above threshold for every cell, so nothing is ever culled and the sculpting step is a no-op. That is a tuning problem, not a refutation — the field-for-patterning argument stands untouched. Left in the codebase, off by default, with the failure written next to the switch rather than the mode quietly deleted.

A HUD that was lying

While verifying, the page read sim 0.0×realtime — which RUNNING.md lists as the signature of a faulted GPU device. The sim was fine. simRate was computed from the local world's steps-per-frame, and in watch mode the local world never steps, so the healthy case was permanently displaying the symptom of the failure case.

It now derives the rate from the server's step counter over wall time. Checked against the server rather than merely made non-zero: HUD 3.7 against a measured 3.41×.

Incidentally the Dev 2.0 world runs faster — 3.41× realtime against 1.22× for Dev 1.0 — because its bodies are smaller in aggregate (26.9k live cells against 37.8k). Nothing was optimised; there is just less world to step.

Still open

  • Patterning. Segments reach 6 but there is no clock and no wavefront. This is the next substantial piece, and cleave mode is probably a prerequisite for the interesting version of it.
  • Muscle is being selected away again — 13.8% down to 7.6% over 126k steps. That is the economy, not the encoding, and it is the same result the 29.4M-step run reported. A new developmental map does not change what the world pays for.
  • Selection response. Canalisation fixed heritability outright (h² 0.093 → 1.000, within-genome variance to exactly zero), but directed selection on morphology still did not climb in the test harness. The remaining suspects are the harness itself — it never preserved the best genome unmutated — and a mutation operator perturbing ~115 of 960 weights per child. Unfinished.
